What If You Had 1000 IQ? You glance at the chessboard once. You already see the next forty moves. Your teacher writes a six digit multiplication on the board. You answer before she drops the chalk. Your friend Leo hands you a tangled charger cable. You hand it back... untangled... in two seconds. The bus timetable, the weather, the crossword, the crowd’s exact head count. Done. Done. Done. Done. And your toast... is still in the toaster.

The average human scores around one hundred on an IQ test. The smartest people ever measured hover near two hundred. You? One thousand. Five times past the edge of the chart.

But here’s the rule. Your brain got faster. Your body didn’t. You can calculate a falling glass perfectly... and still be half a second too slow to catch it.

So what if you had one thousand IQ?

At school, you finish a two hour exam in four minutes. Leo stares at your blank extra time. Dude. You just sat there. I was proving it three more ways, you say. For fun.

Plus: you learn a new language over one lunch period. Grammar, verbs, slang, done by dessert.

Minus: you also notice every tiny lie people tell without meaning to. Your mom says dinner’s almost ready. It is not almost ready. You know this from the pan sounds.

In chess club, you beat the state champion in nine moves. He stares at the board. How... how did you do that? I didn’t, you say. The board did. I just read it.

But where it gets really crazy... is the systems.

You glance at a stock chart once and see the crash three days early. You look at your city’s traffic lights and redesign the timing in your head, cutting the morning jam in half. A real engineer would need a team, and months. You needed a red light.

And there’s a cost nobody warns you about. You can’t turn it off. Ever.

The fridge hum becomes a frequency you calculate instead of ignore. The ceiling fan becomes a geometry problem at two in the morning. You lie there... running numbers you never asked for... while the whole street sleeps.

A normal brain filters out ninety nine percent of what it senses. Yours filters out almost nothing.

Mia, your lab partner, taps your shoulder. You’ve been staring at that wall for an hour. I was mapping the crack pattern, you say. It’s actually kind of beautiful.

That’s the real price. Dopamine, the brain’s little reward spark, fires hardest for surprises. For things you didn’t see coming. But you always see it coming. The movie twist. The pop quiz. The surprise party. All solved in advance. Nothing left... to feel surprised by.

And Leo notices before anyone else does. He’s known you since you were seven. Same table, every lunch, for years. Same dumb knock on your door... three taps, then one. He still does it, even now.

One afternoon he finds you just sitting in the dark. You used to laugh at my terrible jokes, he says quietly. Now you just... solve them before I finish. I miss confusing you.

That hits harder than any equation.

Then an organization shows up. Sharp suits. Soft voices. A man named Mr. Black offers you a lab, a salary, a future. Just solve our market models, he says. Nothing illegal. Just... profitable.

You could predict every trade on Earth by lunch. You could be rich by Friday. The catch? Their numbers don’t add up. You run them twice. Real people lose their savings so the firm wins. Written across one folder, in someone’s shaky hand: Pension. Last savings.

Then the emergency comes. Leo’s apartment building, old wiring, and a spark catches in a stairwell at midnight. You’re three streets away. You calculate the fire’s spread rate instantly... two meters every eight seconds... the exits closing in under two minutes.

You could run the model. Or you could run. The genius thing... or the right thing. You run.

You take the stairs two at a time, dragging a confused family past the smoke, finding Leo by the one window still clear, hauling him out seconds before the hallway folds in on itself.

Outside, soot on your face, Leo coughs out a laugh. Still think I’m not surprising? You actually grin. That one... I didn’t see coming.

You hand Mr. Black’s folder to a reporter instead of his lab. The firm collapses within a week. The savings get returned.

You could have been richer than anyone alive. Instead, you’re just... Leo’s friend again, three knocks and all.

And for the first time in months, his terrible joke actually catches you off guard. You laugh, right on time.

Is any of this real? Nobody has ever scored anywhere near one thousand on an IQ test... the scale basically stops making sense that high. The highest ever recorded, by most estimates, sits closer to two hundred. And real intelligence was never just raw calculation. It’s judgment. It’s knowing what matters. Scientists still argue over what IQ even measures, and whether any number could capture a mind completely.

So could a mind really get that fast... and still stay kind?

That night, your phone buzzes. Unknown number. One line. We know what you did. We have a proposal. You stare at it a long time... then set it face down.

So what would you actually solve first with a mind like that? Tell us the one problem you’d fix by lunchtime.
